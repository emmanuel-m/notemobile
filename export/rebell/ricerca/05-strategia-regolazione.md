## 1. Strategy frameworks: anchor use case, sequencing, super app vs super platform, build/partner/buy, network effects, platform vs aggregator

### Takeaway
Western super apps that work start from one high-frequency, trusted anchor, usually payments. They expand into adjacent higher-margin services, mostly through partners rather than by building in-house. The consensus is that no WeChat-style single super app will emerge in the West soon. Expect an "ecosystem of apps / super platform" model in which payments and finance companies are the most credible orchestrators.

### Cited Findings
- Most super apps start with a high-frequency service (messaging, ride-hailing or payments) and then expand into related areas. Finance is described as a solid foundation for ecosystem expansion — [Adjust, State of super apps](https://www.adjust.com/blog/state-of-super-apps/); [The Paypers, Super apps of today](https://thepaypers.com/payments/thought-leader-insights/super-apps-of-today-the-market-landscape)
- Western apps were generally built for one use case and scaled by adding customers, not by expanding an ecosystem. Western users prefer specialist apps, so all-in-one approaches have had limited success — [Technology Magazine, Is the West on the cusp of embracing the super app era?](https://technologymagazine.com/articles/is-the-west-on-the-cusp-of-embracing-the-super-app-era)
- Deloitte does not expect a single dominant WeChat-like super app in Western markets in the near term. It sees social media, rideshare and payment companies as more likely to succeed than incumbent banks or insurers — [Deloitte, Super apps in the US](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/Industries/financial-services/articles/super-apps-in-the-us.html)
- J.P. Morgan Payments discusses the specific Western super-app model — [J.P. Morgan, Western super apps](https://www.jpmorgan.com/payments/payments-unbound/magazine/articles/western-super-apps) (not fetched in full)
- Swiss proof point for payment-anchored sequencing: TWINT now describes itself as "more than just a payment app… a platform for daily digital life." Its layered features include loyalty cards (Migros Cumulus, Coop Supercard), Super Deals and digital vouchers, subscription comparison, insurance, parking (Digitalparking), ticket presales (Live Nation "TWINT Prio Tickets") and, since 2025, TWINT invoice (eBill-like) — [fintechnews.ch, TWINT 901m transactions](https://fintechnews.ch/payments/twint-901-million-transactions-digital-services-expansion/82118/); [TWINT press](https://www.twint.ch/en/press/twint-makes-switzerlands-payment-infrastructure-more-independent/)
- TWINT scale (2025): 901 million transactions (+~17% vs 773m in 2024), over 6 million users (the mark was passed in August 2025), 77% commercial vs 23% P2P, accepted at ~81% of physical stores and ~86% of online shops in Switzerland. Growth "has slowed markedly" compared with earlier years — [bluewin / Keystone-SDA](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/twint-records-over-900-million-transactions-in-3101722.html); [finews](https://www.finews.com/news/english-news/71219-twint-2025-transaktionen-kunden-2); [TWINT, more than 6 million users](https://www.twint.ch/en/press/more-than-6-million-people-use-twint/). Note: growth is reported as 16.5% by one outlet and 17% by others.
- **[BK]** Gartner defines a "superapp" as an app that provides core features plus access to independently created mini-apps, and calls it a composable platform. In its Top Strategic Technology Trends 2023 it predicted that by 2027 more than 50% of the global population would be daily active users of multiple superapps — [Gartner, What is a superapp?](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/what-is-a-superapp)
- **[BK]** Platform theory: Parker, Van Alstyne and Choudary argue that platforms create value by orchestrating interactions between external producers and consumers ("inverted firm"). The strategic focus moves from controlling resources to orchestrating them, and from optimising internal processes to facilitating external interactions. Network effects (same-side and cross-side) are the main source of value — [HBR, Pipelines, Platforms, and the New Rules of Strategy (2016)](https://hbr.org/2016/04/pipelines-platforms-and-the-new-rules-of-strategy); book *Platform Revolution* (2016)
- **[BK]** Aggregation theory (Ben Thompson, Stratechery): aggregators own the user relationship, face zero marginal cost to serve and commoditise suppliers. Platforms instead enable third parties to build on them (Bill Gates line: a platform creates more value for others than for itself) — [Stratechery, Aggregation Theory](https://stratechery.com/2015/aggregation-theory/)

### Inferences
- Recommended sequencing for a Swiss startup: (1) one high-frequency anchor with daily or weekly use (payments, mobility/parking, loyalty or ticketing are the realistic Swiss options) → (2) adjacent free or low-margin utilities that raise frequency (vouchers, invoices, loyalty wallet) → (3) higher-margin financial and partner services (insurance, credit through partners, investing, subscriptions). TWINT's path shows this pattern and also shows the ceiling: a 6-million-user incumbent owned by the Swiss banks is already occupying the "payment-anchored super app" slot.
- With TWINT holding near-universal payment acceptance, a Swiss startup should probably not compete on payments. It could position as a "super platform" or orchestrator in a non-payment vertical (e.g., mobility, community, Gen Z lifestyle, expat life admin) and integrate TWINT as a payment rail.
- Build/partner/buy heuristic, derived from platform theory and Western evidence: build the anchor and the identity/data layer; partner (mini-apps, white-label, embedded) for regulated or capital-heavy verticals; buy only for capabilities that create network effects or hold licences.
- In Europe, "aggregator" strategies (owning the demand side, commoditising suppliers) attract DMA and antitrust attention once a company reaches scale. A "platform" stance (open APIs, partner-owned brands) is easier to defend with regulators and with partners.

The searches surfaced none, and the a16z "super app" essays (e.g., Connie Chan) were not retrieved. The report writer should not attribute specific frameworks to these firms without sources.
- Gartner's 2027 prediction and the Parker/Van Alstyne and Thompson frameworks were not re-verified in this session.

## 2. Monetisation models and unit economics: what makes super apps profitable

### Takeaway
The best Western evidence is Revolut. Its 2025 profitability came from a diversified fee base (cards/interchange, FX, wealth, and fast-growing subscriptions) on top of interest income, and no single line dominated. Embedded finance is a real but still small revenue pool in Europe.

### Cited Findings
- Revolut FY2025: revenue up 46% to £4.5bn (~$6bn), profit before tax ~$2.3bn (£1.7bn, +57%), net profit ~$1.7bn, the fifth consecutive year of net profitability — [Revolut press release](https://www.revolut.com/en-AT/news/revolut_reports_record_profit_of_2_3bn_for_2025_as_revenue_surges_to_6bn/); [Payment Expert, Revolut 2025 annual report](https://paymentexpert.com/2026/03/24/revolut-2025-annual-report/). One outlet reports +40% revenue growth, which conflicts with the 46% figure elsewhere.
- Revolut subscriptions (paid plans: Plus/Premium/Metal/Ultra) reached £708m (~$936m), +67% YoY from £423m. This makes subscriptions the second-largest revenue line, ahead of Wealth at £663m. Cards brought £1.0bn (+45%) and FX £606m. Fee income was 76% of revenue (72% in 2024). Eleven product lines each exceeded ~£100m — [FXC Intelligence, Revolut 2025 earnings](https://www.fxcintel.com/research/reports/ct-revolut-2025-earnings); [PYMNTS, Revolut profit as subscriptions surge](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3586756). Caveat: Revolut says the revenue components shown do not sum to total group revenue. One secondary analysis instead puts card payments at 22.2% and interest income at 21.6% of revenue, and this was not verified — [Fintech Wrap Up](https://www.fintechwrapup.com/p/deep-dive-an-analytical-breakdown)
- Embedded finance in Europe: cited McKinsey estimate of ~€20–30bn revenue in 2023 (~3% of banking revenue). A BCG estimate puts the North America + Europe TAM at ~$185bn. Both figures come via secondary reporting — [Money20/20 ME, The embedded finance playbook](https://www.insights.money2020middleeast.com/the-embedded-finance-playbook-where-profits-really-come-from/)
- Bain / Bain Capital: US embedded finance transaction value is expected to exceed $7 trillion by 2026 — [Bain press release (2022)](https://www.bain.com/es-ar/about/media-center/press-releases/2022/embedded-finance-transaction-value-to-more-than-double-to-$7-trillion-in-us-by-2026-but-financial-institutions-must-move-quickly-to-keep-upbain--company-and-bain-capital-report/)
- BCG/QED: Monzo reached operational profitability in Europe. Public investors still question fintech profitability, compliance maturity and durability of growth — [BCG & QED, State of Global Fintech 2024 (PDF)](https://thebasispoint.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024-06-State-of-Global-Fintech-BCG-and-QED-Investors.pdf)
- Platform fees on mini-apps: Apple's Mini Apps Partner Program (Nov 2025) cuts the App Store commission to 15% for in-app purchases inside mini apps. This applies if the host uses Apple's purchase-history (Advanced Commerce) API, age-assurance API and in-app purchase flow — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/big-tech/apple-halves-app-store-fees-for-mini-apps-190251153.html); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3228991)
- In the EU, from 1 Oct 2026 Apple replaced the per-install Core Technology Fee (€0.50 per install above 1m) with a 5% "Core Technology Commission" on digital transactions. The CTC also applies to apps distributed through alternative marketplaces and the web. Mini Apps, Video Partner and Small Business programme participants receive additional discounts — [Michael Tsai, New EU App Store terms (18 Aug 2026)](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2026/08/18/new-eu-app-store-terms-to-comply-with-dma/); [basic-tutorials, effective 1 Oct 2026](https://basic-tutorials.com/news/apple-app-store-eu-new-fees-and-rules-effective-october-1-2026/); [TechCentral.ie](https://www.techcentral.ie/apple-adjusts-app-store-commissions-in-eu-to-comply-with-digital-markets-act/)

- Source dates for this section: Revolut FY2025 annual report and press release published around 24 Mar 2026. Apple Mini Apps Partner Program announced 13 Nov 2025 ([CNBC via reader, 13 Nov 2025](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/13/apple-announces-new-program-that-cut-mini-app-fees-in-half.html); [iPhone in Canada, 13 Nov 2025](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/11/13/apple-just-halved-app-store-fees-but-only-for-mini-apps/)). New Apple EU terms effective 1 Oct 2026. Bain embedded-finance press release 2022. BCG/QED report June 2024. Money20/20 ME write-up undated (2025–2026).
- Commentary (opinion, not reporting) argues that with the new EU terms Apple conceded only ~4 percentage points of its 30% commission — [Michael Tsai, 18 Aug 2026](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2026/08/18/new-eu-app-store-terms-to-comply-with-dma/); [Revera Legal](https://www.revera.legal/?p=8145); [Agenda Digitale (IT)](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/mercati-digitali/apple-come-cambiano-i-termini-per-le-app-in-europa-e-cosa-centra-il-dma/)
- 2025 fintech growth: fintech firms grew four times faster than traditional banks in 2025 (BCG report coverage) — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2428949)

### Inferences
- What makes a Western super app profitable is not a single take rate. It is many mid-sized revenue lines on one acquired customer (low CAC per product), and subscriptions are the cleanest margin lever. Revolut's 11 lines above £100m illustrate the "breadth on one relationship" logic.
- For a Swiss startup without a banking licence, a realistic mix is: partner revenue share / referral fees (insurance, credit, investing), merchant fees or affiliate commissions on transactions routed through partners, a premium subscription tier bundling partner perks, and later advertising/retail media on first-party data (with consent). Interest income requires a licence (see §3) or a banking partner.
- Digital goods sold inside mini-apps on iOS bear a 15–30% Apple commission (or a 5% CTC plus other fees in the EU). Physical goods and services (rides, tickets, parking, insurance) are generally outside IAP. This favours real-world services as mini-app verticals. **[BK]** The physical-goods exemption is long-standing App Store Guideline 3.1.3(e)/3.1.5. Verify against the current guidelines.

## 3. Western market barriers: app stores, DMA, PSD3/PSR/FiDA, eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI Wallet, Swiss e-ID, FINMA, nFADP, GDPR, antitrust

### Takeaway
Since 2024 the EU regime has opened distribution and NFC on iOS, and identity and payments rails are converging in 2026–2027 (EUDI Wallets by end 2026, PSD3/PSR adoption expected end 2026). Switzerland is not covered by the DMA or PSD and follows its own track: e-ID from ~1 Dec 2026, and a fintech-licence overhaul into a "payment institution" licence expected no earlier than 2027.

### Cited Findings
**App stores / mini-apps / DMA**
- Apple Mini Apps Partner Program launched Nov 2025: 15% commission for qualifying mini-apps/games hosted inside larger apps, conditioned on Apple APIs (purchase history, age verification, IAP) — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/big-tech/apple-halves-app-store-fees-for-mini-apps-190251153.html); [Michael Tsai blog](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/11/13/mini-apps-partner-program/)
- New EU terms took effect 1 Oct 2026: a 5% Core Technology Commission replaced the €0.50 per-install CTF, and Apple eased the requirements for running alternative iOS marketplaces. The Commission had not confirmed DMA compliance at the time of the sources. In Jan 2026 the Commission opened a separate non-compliance investigation into fees for alternative app stores — [basic-tutorials](https://basic-tutorials.com/news/apple-app-store-eu-new-fees-and-rules-effective-october-1-2026/); [Michael Tsai (Aug 2026)](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2026/08/18/new-eu-app-store-terms-to-comply-with-dma/); [iClarified, Apple accuses EU of delay tactics (Mar 2026)](https://www.iclarified.com/99721/apple-accuses-eu-of-political-delay-tactics-to-justify-app-store-fines)
- A French report says the EU General Court confirmed on 8 July 2026 Apple's DMA gatekeeper designation for the App Store and iOS — [Capmad](https://www.capmad.com/article/sous-pression-du-dma-apple-plie-devant-leurope-et-allege-ses-commissions-sur-lapp-store) (single source, partially read; verify)
- Alternative EU app stores exist (e.g., AltStore, Epic Games Store, Setapp), but uptake is limited. Apple blames the Commission's "delay tactics" for marketplace closures — [TechCrunch (Mar 2025)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/13/move-over-apple-meet-the-alternative-app-stores-coming-to-the-eu); [iDrop News](https://www.idropnews.com/?p=258778)
- The reported ~€500m DMA fine on Apple (April 2025) was NOT confirmed by the results of this session's searches; see Gaps.

**NFC / tap-to-pay**
- The European Commission made Apple's commitments binding in July 2024 (Art. 102 TFEU case, not DMA). Third-party wallets get free NFC access in Host Card Emulation mode without Apple Pay or Wallet, for 10 years across the EEA, under FRAND eligibility criteria, with a monitoring trustee — [EC decision summary, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2024/1027/oj/eng); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/44443/apple-reaches-deal-with-ec-on-nfc-payments-access); [NFCW](https://www.nfcw.com/?p=88501)
- First uses: Vipps MobilePay (Norway, late 2024) and Curve (EU iOS NFC wallet) — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/theres-now-an-apple-pay-alternative-for-iphone-users-in-norway-153634514.html); [Finextra, Curve](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46025/curve-wallet-to-compete-with-apple-pay-on-ios-devices)
- NFC timeline detail: Apple offered the commitments on 19 Jan 2024 (EU market test). They were accepted and made binding on 11 Jul 2024. They apply to developers established in the EEA and to users with an EEA-registered Apple ID — [TechCrunch, 19 Jan 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/19/apple-pay-eu-commitments/); [fintech.global, 11 Jul 2024](https://fintech.global/2024/07/11/apple-avoids-massive-eu-fines-by-opening-nfc-technology-to-third-party-providers/); [Payments Industry Intelligence](https://paymentsindustryintelligence.com/ec-accepts-commitments-by-apple-to-open-nfc-on-iphones/). Sources disagree on the legal basis: some attribute Curve's launch to the DMA, others to the Art. 102 commitments. The Art. 102 commitments decision is the primary basis.
- Switzerland (non-EEA) is NOT covered by the EEA NFC commitments. **[BK]** Apple opened NFC/SE APIs in other regions from iOS 18.1 (e.g., US, UK, Japan) under commercial terms with fees. TWINT works on iPhone with QR codes rather than tap-to-pay for that reason. Verify the current Swiss status.

**PSD3 / PSR / FiDA (EU open banking / open finance)**
- Provisional political agreement on PSD3 + PSR was reached on 27 Nov 2025. Coreper approved the compromise on 22 Apr 2026 and the EP ECON committee on 5 May 2026 — [banking.vision](https://banking.vision/?p=42087); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/fintech/1780650/eu-reaches-compromise-on-psd3-and-psr-tighter-harmonisation-crypto-rules-and-stronger-consumer-transparency); [Council doc ST-8220-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8220-2026-INIT/en/pdf)
- As of October 2026, publication in the Official Journal is expected by Dec 2026 and PSD3/PSR are not yet in force. PSR general application is ~21 months after entry into force, and Verification of Payee provisions apply at ~27 months, which implies roughly H2 2028 — [Adyen knowledge hub, PSD3](https://www.adyen.com/knowledge-hub/psd3); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/cedd39c6/psd3-and-psr-from-provisional-agreement-to-2026-readiness); [DLA Piper (Mar 2026)](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/psd3-and-psr). Content highlights (consent/permission dashboards, anti-fraud and IBAN/name-check, liability for impersonation fraud) — [Capco](https://www.capco.com/intelligence/capco-intelligence/psd3-psr-the-council-of-the-eu-proposal-on-the-consent-panel-and-fraud)
- FiDA (Financial Data Access, open finance beyond payment accounts) was still in trilogue as of April 2026. Scenarios have agreement slipping to 2027 and application around 2029–2030 — [banking.vision](https://banking.vision/?p=42087); [Finologee](https://finologee.com/the-next-evolution-of-eu-payments-an-update-on-psd3-and-psr/). I found no confirmation of a FiDA deal by 1 Nov 2026.

**eIDAS 2.0 / EU Digital Identity Wallet**
- Under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, each Member State must provide at least one certified EUDI Wallet by late 2026 (cited cutoff 24 Dec 2026). Private relying parties that must use strong customer authentication (banks, payments, AML-obliged entities) must accept the wallets by end-2027. Very Large Online Platforms must accept them when users request it. Relying parties must register nationally and declare which data they request and why — [Arthur Cox](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/the-eu-digital-identity-wallet-what-companies-need-to-know/); [Kennedys](https://kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/the-european-digital-identity-framework-introducing-the-new-eu-digital-identity-wallet/); [fintech.global (Jul 2026)](https://fintech.global/2026/07/13/eidas-2-0-deadlines-loom-two-tracks-one-trust-chain/); [EU Perspectives (Jan 2026)](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/01/eu-digital-identity-wallet-fces-2026-deadline/). Readiness across the EU-27 is uneven, with Member States split into three readiness tiers — [b2trust, Q2 2026 status](https://b2trust.com/en/blog/eidas-2-implementation-status-eu-27-q2-2026). The 24-month deadline runs from adoption of the implementing acts. France, Austria and Italy are cited as having launched national EUDI wallet implementations ahead of the formal deadline. One source says public-sector relying parties must accept notified wallets from the same 24 Dec 2026 cutoff — [Gataca, eIDAS2 timeline](https://gataca.io/resources/blog/eIDAS2-timeline); [compliancehub calendar](https://calendar.compliancehub.wiki/deadline/eu-digital-identity-wallet-availability-2026); [walt.id](https://walt.id/eidas2). Payment providers are still working through ambiguities in how eIDAS interacts with SCA — [Biometric Update, May 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202505/digital-id-payments-providers-are-trying-to-solve-eidas-ambiguities). Most of these sources are law firms or vendors; check against Reg. (EU) 2024/1183 and its implementing acts.

**Swiss e-ID (swiyu)**
- The e-ID Act was approved by referendum on 28 Sep 2025 with 50.39% Yes and 49.61% No, a margin of ~21,266 votes, on 49.55% turnout — [eid.admin.ch](https://www.eid.admin.ch/en/e-id-gesetz-an-der-urne-angenommen-e); [admin.ch popular vote](https://www.admin.ch/en/popular-vote-on-28-september-2025); [bluewin](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/switzerland/great-relief-in-the-yes-camp-confederation-can-implement-e-id-li.2892431)

A CHF 1.7m budget cut for 2026 suspended international interoperability, a federal backup service and issuance into third-party wallets. Complaints against the vote result are pending at the Federal Supreme Court, and the Federal Council will await the ruling before enacting the Act — [IDTechWire](https://idtechwire.com/switzerland-delays-national-e-id-launch-to-december-2026-amid-security-and-acceptance-concerns/); [Biometric Update (Mar 2026)](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202603/swiss-e-id-delayed-to-december-renewed-focus-on-security-and-trustworthiness); [heise](https://heise.de/-11193786); [iamexpat.ch](https://www.iamexpat.ch/expat-info/swiss-news/e-id-coming-switzerland-december-1)
- More detail on the vote and roll-out: without the votes of Swiss abroad, Yes would have been only 50.14%. Swiss Review gives 50.34%, which conflicts with the official 50.39% and is likely an error — [Swiss Review](https://www.swisscommunity.org/en/news-media/swiss-revue/article/switzerland-to-introduce-e-id). - Privacy-by-design rules for relying parties: only legally authorised providers may query the AHV number (unauthorised requests are blocked by the swiyu wallet). Providers must deposit their data queries and purpose in a public federal register in advance — [IDTechWire](https://idtechwire.com/switzerland-delays-national-e-id-launch-to-december-2026-amid-security-and-acceptance-concerns/)

**FINMA / Swiss licensing**

**Data protection / consent / antitrust**
- **[BK]** The Swiss revised Federal Act on Data Protection (nFADP/revDSG) has been in force since 1 Sep 2023. It requires privacy by design and by default, a duty to inform, DPIAs for high-risk processing, and criminal fines of up to CHF 250,000 on responsible individuals. Its approach is generally less consent-centric than the GDPR, but express consent is needed for sensitive data and high-risk profiling — [FDPIC/EDÖB](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/en)
- **[BK]** GDPR: consent must be freely given, specific and granular (Art. 7 and Art. 4(11); EDPB Guidelines 05/2020). Bundling consent across services or making a service conditional on unnecessary data processing undermines validity. This matters for cross-service data combination in a super app — [EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-052020-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en)
- **[BK]** DMA Art. 5(2) bars designated gatekeepers from combining personal data across core platform services without consent. This only applies to gatekeepers (≥€7.5bn EU turnover or ≥€75bn market cap and ≥45m monthly EU users), so it does not apply to a startup. It does, however, set the regulatory expectation — [EU DMA official page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/about-dma_en)
- Analysts note that Europe's strict privacy and competition regime complicates super-app adoption — [IADS, Superapps in Europe: pros and cons](https://www.iads.org/member/articles-reports/superapps-in-europe-pros-and-cons)

### Inferences
- A Swiss startup must design for two regimes: Swiss law at home (nFADP, FINMA, e-ID, no DMA or PSD rights), and EU law if it serves EU users (GDPR, PSD3/PSR, eIDAS, DMA-enabled iOS distribution and NFC). The EU-only iOS openings (alternative stores, HCE NFC, external payment links) do NOT automatically apply to Swiss Apple IDs. A Swiss iOS strategy should therefore assume the standard App Store rules plus the Mini Apps Partner Program.
- Licensing path: start licence-light (partner with a bank or licensed payment institution, act as an introducer or tied agent), and reassess when the new payment-institution licence becomes available, expected ≥2027.
- Identity: plan to accept swiyu e-ID for onboarding/KYC from Dec 2026–2027. Note that private reuse is constrained by the public register of data queries and purposes. For EU users, the EUDI Wallet becomes the standard onboarding credential in 2027.
- Consent architecture is a product decision: per-service, granular, revocable consent with a single permission dashboard. PSD3/PSR is moving banks toward a similar consent dashboard. This lets the app combine data across mini-apps lawfully and turns compliance into trust UX.
- Antitrust and bundling risk is low at startup scale but grows with dominance. Avoid tying (forcing use of own payment or own services in partner mini-apps) and keep partner terms FRAND-like.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the April 2025 ~€500m DMA fine on Apple (or the €200m Meta fine) in this session. Verify on the Commission's DMA press pages.
- I could not confirm the final Official Journal publication of PSD3/PSR or a FiDA agreement as of 1 Nov 2026.
- I found no 2025–2026 primary text on Google Play's mini-app or DMA terms.
- I found no confirmation of Apple NFC/HCE access terms in Switzerland.

## 4. Trust as a strategic asset: privacy expectations, consumer surveys, generational differences

### Takeaway
European demand for all-in-one apps is real but held by a minority (~40% highly interested at best), and it depends on security and provider reputation. Gen Z and millennials are the most receptive. Survey data is old (2022–2023) and mostly PYMNTS-led.

### Cited Findings
- PYMNTS/PayPal 2022 (US, UK, Australia, Germany): 72% of consumers were interested in a super app. "High" interest ("very/extremely interested") was reported by 25% overall, 40% of millennials, 35% of Gen X and only 25% of Gen Z. In Germany, 20% were very or extremely interested — [PYMNTS, 72% of consumers interested](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/the-data-point-72-percent-consumers-interested-super-apps/); [PYMNTS report PDF (Oct–Nov 2022)](https://www.pymnts.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PYMNTS-Super-Apps-For-The-Super-Connected-October-November-2022.pdf); [PYMNTS, 76% of millennials super app ready](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=1431271)
- UK consumers trust PayPal more than banks to provide a super app. In Germany, consumers see banks as super-app providers — [PYMNTS UK](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/uk-consumers-trust-paypal-more-than-banks-to-provide-super-app-study-finds/); [PYMNTS Germany](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=1381210). Card networks are preferred over banks for super-app delivery — [Payments Industry Intelligence](https://paymentsindustryintelligence.com/card-networks-preferred-over-banks-for-super-app-delivery/)

### Inferences
- In Switzerland, trust is the binding constraint: the narrow e-ID vote shows deep scepticism about centralised digital identity and data even when the state runs it. A super app positioned as "Swiss-hosted, data-minimising, no data sale, granular consent" turns compliance into differentiation. Swiss ownership and hosting are a plausible counterweight to the 84% EU distrust of US Big Tech.
- Gen Z is a good beachhead because of higher willingness to trade data for convenience. The super-app proposition must still be utility-led ("one app for X"), not "super app"-branded, since stated high interest is around 25–40%.
- The most trusted brands to host a super app are payment providers (PayPal in the UK, TWINT in CH) and, in some markets, banks. A startup may need to borrow trust through partner brands (white-label, co-branding with a trusted Swiss institution).

## 5. Partnerships / B2B2C: white-label mini-apps, embedded finance, loyalty coalitions (European examples)

### Takeaway
In Europe the super app is assembled through partnerships: embedded finance, mini-apps inside a host, and loyalty coalitions. TWINT's integrations with Migros, Coop, Live Nation and Digitalparking are the clearest Swiss model, and Apple now has an explicit commercial frame (15%) for mini-app hosting.

### Cited Findings
- TWINT B2B2C: loyalty cards (Migros Cumulus, Coop Supercard) stored in the app, partner offers and Super Deals, insurance, parking via Digitalparking, Live Nation presales, a Business Portal app for merchants, and TWINT invoice — [fintechnews.ch](https://fintechnews.ch/payments/twint-901-million-transactions-digital-services-expansion/82118/); [TWINT about us](https://www.twint.ch/en/about-us/)
- Apple Mini Apps Partner Program (Nov 2025) creates a formal channel for hosting third-party HTML5/JS mini-apps in a native app at a 15% commission, with conditions — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/big-tech/apple-halves-app-store-fees-for-mini-apps-190251153.html); [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/apple-launches-a-new-mini-app-partner-program-with-reduced-fees-new-requirements/)
- Embedded banking projected to generate €626bn for European brands by 2028 (trade press figure) — [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi_prime_news/heres-how-embedded-banking-solutions-will-generate-e626bn-for-european-brands-by-2028). Capgemini frames embedded finance as a ~$500bn opportunity — [Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/us-en/insights/expert-perspectives/embedded-finance-unlocking-the-500bn-opportunity/). European businesses show a "more conservative approach" to embedded finance projects, and Europe lags on merchant-native integrations — [Money20/20 ME](https://www.insights.money2020middleeast.com/the-embedded-finance-playbook-where-profits-really-come-from/)
- Vipps MobilePay (Nordics) and Curve show the "wallet as aggregator" model enabled by NFC openings — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/theres-now-an-apple-pay-alternative-for-iphone-users-in-norway-153634514.html); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46025/curve-wallet-to-compete-with-apple-pay-on-ios-devices)

### Inferences
- Partnership options for a Swiss startup:
  - (a) Become a mini-app host: open an SDK for partner mini-apps such as mobility, ticketing, insurance or local commerce; use Apple's 15% program for digital goods; and keep physical-world services outside IAP.
  - (b) Embed partners' regulated products (bank or payment institution BaaS, insurers) under revenue share.
  - (c) Plug into existing loyalty coalitions (Cumulus, Supercard) rather than building a points currency.
  - (d) Integrate TWINT and e-ID as rails rather than compete with them.
- Asian contrast (WeChat Mini Programs, Alipay, Grab) is useful only to show that hosts there control the OS-like layer. In Europe the OS gatekeepers (Apple/Google) and regulation keep that layer contested, so the host must win on brand and trust rather than distribution lock-in.
