## Q1. What UX problems come from packing many services into one app?

### Takeaway
The best-documented problems are: (a) navigation/IA overload and hidden services (NN/g: hidden navigation cuts discoverability almost in half); (b) home-screen clutter that users voice explicitly (Revolut forum: "23 buttons/links on home screen"; Paytm declutter redesigns in 2018, 2022 and 2025); (c) cosmetic redesigns that fail without IA work (Gojek, 38M users); (d) UI inconsistency across services and mini-apps (Gojek before Asphalt, and WeChat imposing no unified navigation bar); (e) blurred permission/privacy boundaries between host and mini-program (Citizen Lab 2023, academic papers); (f) backlash against forced unbundling (Messenger 2014). Quantitative evidence specific to super apps is scarce. The bulk comes from general HCI work (NN/g), company accounts and user anecdotes.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Navigation, discoverability, cognitive load
- NN/g, quantitative study on hidden vs. visible navigation: "discoverability is cut almost in half by hiding a website's main navigation". Task time increases and perceived task difficulty rises — [NN/g video "Hamburger Menus Hurt UX Metrics"](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/hamburger-menus/) (n.d., series 2016)
- NN/g: on desktop, hiding navigation degrades experience and discoverability more than on phones, but "hiding global navigation degrades the user experience both on desktop and on mobile" — [NN/g "Find navigation on mobile, even hamburger"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-mobile-even-hamburger/) (n.d., ~2016); [NN/g "Find navigation on desktop: not hamburger"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-desktop-not-hamburger/) (n.d., ~2016)
- NN/g, salience: one tested site had "inconspicuous, low-salience navigation that was easy to ignore", and participants did not use it. Being visible is not enough; navigation must stand out — [NN/g desktop](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-desktop-not-hamburger/)
- NN/g guidance: use visible or combination navigation instead of hiding it in a menu, and label the menu, because "the hamburger menu is not so universal that everyone knows it or immediately looks for it". NN/g's caveat: "usability guidelines are always circumstantial", so test with your own users — [NN/g "Hamburger Menus"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/) (n.d.)
- Unverified claim: a test where the hamburger icon was clicked 20% less than a "Menu" button (Digiday, unsourced; repeated by a 2026 blog). Low confidence — [Digiday "The problem with the hamburger menu"](https://digiday.com/publishers/the-problem-with-the-hamburger-menu/) (n.d.); [Girard Media / Digital Trends](https://girardmedia.com/blog/progressive-disclosure-ux-complexity-management) (2026)
- ProCreator (design agency, vendor blog): "Challenge #1" of super apps is navigation overload, with "progressive navigation" as the fix — essential actions one tap away, advanced services deeper. Also: re-skin third-party services to match the host's buttons, typography and navigation patterns — [ProCreator "How to solve super app design challenges"](https://procreator.design/blog/how-to-solve-super-app-design-challenges/) (n.d.)
- UX audit of Indian neobanks ("super-app paradox"): each added feature adds mental load and erodes the sense of safety and clarity banking needs. It cites Hick's Law and Miller's Law ("in every UX textbook for 30 years") — [A. Chinchakar, "Super-app paradox in neobanks"](https://portfolio-adichinchakar.vercel.app/writing/super-app-paradox-neobanks) (n.d.; individual portfolio, low authority)
- Cognitive load theory applied to apps: separate intrinsic load (the task) from extraneous load ("wasted mental energy that comes from unclear buttons, hidden navigation, or confusing layouts"). Design should cut the extraneous part — [Glance "How can I reduce cognitive load in my app interface"](https://thisisglance.com/learning-centre/how-can-i-reduce-cognitive-load-in-my-app-interface) (n.d., vendor)
- When the IA does not match user intent, cognitive load and task abandonment rise — [Lollypop, "Mobile app redesign case study"](https://lollypop.design/blog/2025/november/mobile-app-redesign-case-study-improve-ux/) (Nov 2025, vendor)

- Critical commentary on Revolut and hyper-personalisation — [Ergomania "Will it be enough to upgrade Revolut in the age of hyper-personalisation?"](https://ergomania.eu/will-it-be-enough-to-upgrade-revolut-in-the-age-of-hyper-personalisation) (n.d., ~2024; content not retrieved)
- **Paytm 2018**: home screen simplified to "make it simpler and let you focus on some of the most used Paytm features" — [Paytm blog "Experience the all new Paytm app"](https://medium.com/paytm-blog/experience-the-all-new-paytm-app-386895b71f2) (2018); [CIOL](https://www.ciol.com/paytm-revamps-app-intuitive-ui) (n.d.); [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/business/report-paytm-revamp-its-app-to-take-on-competition-here-s-what-has-changed-2600267) (2018)
- **Paytm 2022**: new homepage that "better supports Talkback on Android" (accessibility) — [Paytm blog "Say hello to the new Paytm homepage"](https://paytm.com/blog/whats-new/say-hello-to-the-new-paytm-homepage) (2022)
- **Paytm November 2025**: "cleaner" relaunch with a lighter palette and redesigned home layout. Business Today: icons and sections reorganised "making navigation smoother". Tribune: "limited its entire interface to two scrolls, representing a clutter-free and simple design" — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/paytm-revamps-its-app-with-ai-powered-tools-digital-gold-rewards-and-cleaner-interface-501649-2025-11-11) (11 Nov 2025); [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/paytm-launches-all-new-consumer-centric-app-with-digital-gold-rewards-on-every-payment-and-ai-led-features) (Nov 2025); [India.com](https://www.india.com/technology/paytm-launches-new-app-experience-with-cleaner-interface-and-smart-payment-features-8176497/) (Nov 2025); [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/paytm-app-gets-major-redesign-with-ai-features-new-rewards-and-smarter-tools-2025-11-11-1016841) (11 Nov 2025)

The proposal cuts this to about 8 focused actions. These are the author's figures, not Paytm data — [S. Bhuyan, Paytm Case Study](https://sristibhuyan1-cpu.github.io/Paytm-Case-Study/) (2026)
- **Grab, pre-2018**: before the superapp redesign, the app had "cluttered pins, icons and texts". The redesign removed them while keeping the green/white scheme — [Top Gear PH](https://www.topgear.com.ph/news/motoring-news/new-and-improved-grab-app-a00013-20180508) (8 May 2018)

#### C. Cosmetic redesign without IA research: Gojek
- Gojek had just redesigned the homepage of an app used by 38M people and "went viral" for user dissatisfaction (June 2022 post). Rahul Bhatli, Consumer Platform Design Lead, was brought in to understand why users were unhappy with a multi-year homepage redesign — [Looppanel "We went viral for the wrong reasons"](https://looppanel.com/blog/we-went-viral-for-the-wrong-reasons) (June 2022; vendor case story, no primary Gojek confirmation)
- What failed: moving components, changing the placement of search, reordering menu options, tracking CTR and navigation journeys. "Even after changing the entire layout of the homepage multiple times, there was no significant change in user behavior or business metrics" — [Looppanel](https://looppanel.com/blog/we-went-viral-for-the-wrong-reasons) (June 2022)
- Indonesian academic paper on Gojek UX after v4.0: user complaints may come from "three causes" (content not retrieved) — [Wimba: Jurnal Komunikasi Visual, ITB](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/wimba/article/view/16448) (2022)
- Other Indonesian studies on convenience/accessibility of Gojek service features (content not retrieved) — [LPKIA JTI](https://jurnal.lpkia.ac.id/index.php/jti/article/view/603) (n.d.); [LPKIA JKB "Gojek app: user comfort and accessibility"](https://jurnal.lpkia.ac.id/index.php/jkb/article/view/572) (n.d.); [UIB repository, usability analysis](https://repository.uib.ac.id/2419) (n.d.)
- Analysis of Gojek Google Play reviews (strengths and areas for improvement) — [Kimola report](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-gojeks-user-feedback-secrets-for-growth-google-play-id-id-154591) (n.d.)

#### D. UI inconsistency across services, teams and mini-apps
- Gojek 2018: the Head of Design (Abhinit Tiwari) had to redesign all 16 services in 8 months, which prompted the Asphalt design system (with Design Systems Lead Mayank Sagar) — [Gojek blog "Of dreams, deadlines and a design system"](https://blog.gojek.io/of-dreams-deadlines-and-a-design-system) (2018-19); [UX Collective version](https://uxdesign.cc/of-dreams-deadlines-and-a-design-system-baf14eb9ad1d) (n.d.)
- Gojek: crashes in GO-SEND halved after it used components from GO-RIDE. Shared components cut both inconsistency and bugs — [Inc42 "How Gojek's design system was a painkiller for the org"](https://inc42.com/resources/how-gojeks-design-system-was-a-painkiller-for-the-org) (n.d.)
- WeChat: "WeChat does not provide a unified navigation bar style for mini programs". Developers design navigation for home and secondary pages. Only the official top-right menu is fixed. This structurally creates inconsistency across mini-programs — [WeChat Mini Program Design Guidelines](https://developers.weixin.qq.com/miniprogram/en/design/) (n.d., official)
- UTAUT study of college students in the Pearl River Delta: some users find opening Mini Programs inconvenient, which weakens the experience — [Shenzhen University, Lìyán Forum](https://ma.szu.edu.cn/info/2528/5329.htm) (n.d.)

#### E. Trust, permissions, privacy across services
- Citizen Lab, "Should We Chat? Privacy in the WeChat Ecosystem": every Mini Program is enrolled in usage tracking, so activity data goes to WeChat as well as the developer — [Citizen Lab full report](https://citizenlab.ca/2023/06/privacy-in-the-wechat-ecosystem-full-report/) (June 2023)
- Citizen Lab research note: permission boundaries between Mini Programs and the host app are unclear, and granting location to a mini-program also sends geolocation to WeChat — [Citizen Lab](https://citizenlab.ca/?p=79480) (2023)
- Academic paper: a crawler indexed over 1.3M mini-apps and studied permission management across host app ecosystems. It found at least one security vulnerability due to improper permission management in the hosts — [NSF PAR 10290097](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10290097) (~2021)
- arXiv: on Android, WeChat MiniApp third-party developers can access user data, often without clear consent, and current safeguards are inadequate — [arXiv 2401.03218](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03218v1) (Jan 2024)
- UTAUT2 + privacy survey (n=251) of an instant-messaging/social super app: privacy concerns reduce the effects of performance expectancy and effort expectancy. The authors recommend explicit privacy policies — [CIHE repository](https://repository.cihe.edu.hk/jspui/handle/cihe/879?mode=full) (2015)

#### F. Notification fatigue (general evidence only)
- *Information Systems Research*: data from 200+ push campaigns on a US mobile-offer marketplace shows push significantly increases same-day click-throughs to advertised merchants (the benefit side of push, not the fatigue side) — [Campaigns That Click, ISR, INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2024.0917) (2024-25)
- Vendor claims, secondhand and low confidence: 52% of users who disable push eventually churn (Localytics), and apps with full preference controls see 43% fewer opt-outs (Customer.io) — [Courier blog](https://www.courier.com/blog/how-to-reduce-notification-fatigue-7-proven-product-strategies-for-saas) (n.d.)
- Vendor guidance on alert fatigue and push fatigue — [SuprSend](https://www.suprsend.com/post/alert-fatigue) (n.d.); [ContextSDK](https://contextsdk.com/blogposts/avoiding-push-fatigue-common-user-turn-offs) (n.d.)

#### G. Backlash against forced unbundling: Facebook Messenger
- In 2014 Facebook removed chat from the main app and forced users to download Messenger. Users were "annoyed at having to download another app in order to use what had been one of the core features"; reviews on both stores were "overwhelmingly negative" — hundreds of negative reviews, plus secondary privacy/permission concerns — [Motley Fool "Messenger dominates despite poor reviews"](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2014/09/02/facebook-messenger-dominates-despite-poor-reviews.aspx) (2 Sep 2014); [The Next Web, Facebook responds to Messenger backlash](https://thenextweb.com/news/facebook-responds-messenger-backlash-news-feed-alert-explanation-mobile-apps) (27 Aug 2014); [TruTower](https://trutower.com/?p=20428) (2014)
- Zuckerberg (Q&A, November 2014): messages were buried — "in order to get to it you had to wait for the app to load and go to a separate tab". "Each app can only focus on doing one thing well." He called the switch "painful" in the short term — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2014/11/06/zuckerberg-explains-separate-messenger-download/) (6 Nov 2014); [Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/facebook-s-zuckerberg-explains-why-users-were-forced-to-download-separate-messenger-app) (Nov 2014); [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/Mark-Zuckerberg-Reveals-Why-You-Were-Forced-To-Download-Facebooks-Separate-Messenger-App/articleshow/45073683.cms) (Nov 2014); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2014/11/10/facebook-explains-why-it-split-messenger-from-main-mobile-app) (10 Nov 2014)
- A critic argued the change "isn't in the best interest of users" and served the company — [Motley Fool "Will the Facebook Messenger move hurt…"](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2014/08/13/will-the-facebook-messenger-move-hurt-the-social-m.aspx) (13 Aug 2014)
- Outcome: Messenger users more than doubled from 200M (April 2014), and by July 2016 it had passed 1B users "despite confusion" — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2014/09/10/why-facebook-inc-needed-a-separate-messaging-app.aspx) (10 Sep 2014); [Techspective](https://techspective.net/2016/07/20/facebook-messenger-crosses-billion-user-mark-despite-confusion/) (20 Jul 2016)

### Inferences
- A recurring cycle across cases: services get added, clutter accumulates, then a "simplify" redesign follows (Paytm 2018 → 2022 → 2025; Revolut v8 → v10; Grab pre-2018 → 2018). The Revolut forum's cross-sell/advertising complaint suggests a root cause in business-unit governance (competition for home real estate), not only visual design.
- Gojek shows clutter is a mental-model/IA problem. Rearranging tiles without changing the service taxonomy does not move metrics.
- Privacy in super apps is structural: one consent can serve two parties (host + mini-program). A heuristic worth adding: "at permission time, state who receives the data".
- Messenger shows that unbundling can win on usage while losing on trust and sentiment in the short term. The cost is concentrated in the migration method (forced) rather than in the split itself.

### Gaps
- No Baymard or NN/g study specific to super apps or mini-programs was found. NN/g evidence covers navigation in general.
- No CHI/ACM paper on super app usability was found in the searches.
- No quantitative data specific to super apps was found on: notification fatigue, the effect of app size/performance on UX, brand dilution, accessibility (except Paytm's TalkBack improvement and WeChat's age-friendly guide), or multilingual content.
- The Wimba (ITB) paper on Gojek complaints after v4.0 was found, but the "three causes" were not retrieved.

## Q2. Which UX patterns and solutions address these problems?

### Takeaway
The documented solutions fall into these families: (1) a home with pinned/customisable favourites plus an "All services" hub (Revolut v8/v10); (2) radical decluttering with a scroll limit, aggregated views and OS widgets that do the job outside the app (Paytm 2025); (3) a services overview on home in place of the single-service map, while removing the sidebar (Grab 2018); (4) IA rebuilt from real behaviour (Gojek); (5) a host design system and mini-program guidelines with fixed host chrome and freedom inside (WeChat capsule, Alipay's four principles, Gojek Asphalt); (6) feeds and personalised surfacing (Grab); (7) in 2025-2026, a conversational AI agent as the orchestration layer that calls mini-programs as "skills" (Grab AI Assistant, Alipay Abao and AI Pay, WeChat agent). Unbundling (Messenger, Gojek GoBiz for merchants, PhonePe Pincode) is the alternative when a service or audience needs focus.

### Inferences
- Pattern hierarchy for the checklist: (1) 4-8 pinned/personalised services on home + an "All services" hub (Revolut); (2) fixed, recognisable host chrome (WeChat capsule) with partner freedom inside; (3) a design system mandatory for first-party services (Asphalt) and guideline-level for third parties (WeChat/Alipay four principles); (4) jobs done outside the app via OS widgets (Paytm QR); (5) a scroll limit as a declutter constraint (Paytm "two scrolls"); (6) agent/chat as an optional alternative entry point, not a replacement (WeChat agent optional, opened by a swipe).
- The AI-agent trend turns mini-programs into back-end "skills". The UX focus shifts from the tile grid to agent transparency, confirmation before payment (identity verification in AI Pay), and partner service quality. Alipay's failed separate app (Zhi Xiao Bao 2024) next to an agent embedded inside Alipay (Abao 2026) suggests the agent works better embedded in the existing super app. This is an inference, not a sourced claim.
- Unbundling by audience (Gojek GoBiz for merchants) is less controversial than unbundling a core consumer feature (Messenger). If a split is made, migrate gradually and keep cross-app deep links.
- Alipay's guidelines (consistency with the merchant's own app) and WeChat's (consistency with the host's conventions) represent two different models of brand balance: brand-first (Alipay/KFC) vs platform-first (WeChat capsule). This is a useful framing for the "brand dilution" problem.

## Q3. Which metrics and research methods suit super app UX?

### Takeaway
Google's HEART framework (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success; Goals → Signals → Metrics) is the most cited structure and works well per service cohort in a super app. Company cases show A/B experiments on conversion and bounce (Grab), tracking of CTR and navigation journeys (Gojek, which failed when used alone), IA tested with real users on the live app (Gojek), and usage data plus feedback (Satispay). Academia uses UTAUT/UTAUT2 for acceptance, and NN/g uses discoverability, task time and perceived difficulty for navigation.

### Cited Findings
- HEART: created by Google's UX research team (Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson, Xin Fu). The five categories are Happiness (satisfaction via surveys such as CSAT and CES, often as in-app polls), Engagement ("how often and how deeply users interact"), Adoption (new users over a time window), Retention (D1/D7/D30 curves, monthly active retention, gross/net revenue retention for B2B) and Task Success (task completion, search exits, crash rates) — [IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/google-s-heart-framework-for-measuring-ux) (n.d.); [Lyssna](https://www.lyssna.com/blog/google-heart-framework/) (26 Mar 2026); [Koji](https://www.koji.so/docs/heart-framework-ux-metrics) (2026); [Product Compass](https://www.productcompass.pm/p/the-google-heart-framework) (n.d.)
- HEART is applied through Goals, Signals and Metrics. The original paper recommends picking only the categories that map to the project goal — [Amplitude](https://www.amplitude.com/blog/heart-framework-software-ux.md) (n.d.); [Usersnap](https://usersnap.com/blog/heart-framework/) (n.d.); [Simon Hilgers](https://simonhilgers.substack.com/p/increase-the-quality-of-your-ux-with) (n.d.)
- Grab: homepage revamps measured with experiments on conversion and bounce rate, with UI/UX experiments run routinely — [Grab editorial](https://www.grab.com/sg/wp-json/wp/v2/editorial/211839) (n.d.)
- Grab 2018: the hypothesis was measured as service conversion rate, discoverability and visibility of services on home — [Grab redesign case study](https://rice-temporary-site.squarespace.com/grabredesign) (n.d.)
- Gojek: CTR and navigation journeys tracked across layout variants showed no movement, so the team switched to behaviour-based IA modelling with real-user testing on the live app. Looppanel also reports using research to boost driver feature adoption at Gojek — [Looppanel](https://looppanel.com/blog/we-went-viral-for-the-wrong-reasons) (June 2022); [casestudies.com, Gojek driver feature adoption](https://www.casestudies.com/company/looppanel/case-study/gojek-customer-case-study) (n.d.)
- NN/g measured navigation using discoverability, task time and perceived task difficulty — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/hamburger-menus/) (n.d.)
- Academia: UTAUT for Mini Program acceptance, UTAUT2 + privacy concern for super-app continuance, and a study of purchase-intention factors on a multi-service platform (Gojek, Aceh) — [Shenzhen Univ.](https://ma.szu.edu.cn/info/2528/5329.htm) (n.d.); [CIHE](https://repository.cihe.edu.hk/jspui/handle/cihe/879?mode=full) (2015); [IIUM student repository](https://studentrepo.iium.edu.my/entities/publication/e3c11241-3a7c-4958-99f0-43a92ec42299/full) (n.d.)
- App-store review analysis by theme as a qualitative signal — [Kimola, Gojek report](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-gojeks-user-feedback-secrets-for-growth-google-play-id-id-154591) (n.d.)
- Satispay: concepts validated through app usage data plus user feedback — [Built In](https://builtin.com/job/product-designer/8057932) (n.d.)
- Push: same-day click-through to merchants as an outcome metric for push campaigns — [ISR/INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2024.0917) (2024-25). Opt-out rate and churn after push disable as fatigue indicators (vendor figures: 52% churn, -43% opt-out with preference controls, low confidence) — [Courier](https://www.courier.com/blog/how-to-reduce-notification-fatigue-7-proven-product-strategies-for-saas) (n.d.)

### Inferences
- A proposed super app metric set: HEART per service cohort; cross-service breadth (number of services used per MAU over 30/90 days); time to first use of a second service; home-tile CTR vs search use (signals discoverability trouble); retention by entry service; cannibalisation (does promoting one service reduce use of another?); push opt-out rate by service; task success and time-on-task in navigation tests; SUS/UMUX-Lite for overall perception; review sentiment by theme. For AI agents: task completion rate, handoff-to-GUI rate, confirmation/undo rate before payment.
- Methods: card sorting and tree testing to rebuild the service taxonomy (the Gojek lesson); first-click tests on the home grid; diary studies for cross-service journeys; permission-comprehension tests ("who receives this data?") for privacy; A/B tests only after IA validation.

### Gaps
- No published super-app benchmarks were found (typical SUS, cross-service adoption rates, cannibalisation figures). Use of SUS/UMUX in super app studies was not found.
- No company-published HEART implementation for a super app was found.
- No Deloitte/Accenture report on super app UX was found within the search budget.
